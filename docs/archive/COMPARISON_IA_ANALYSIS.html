Where do comparison tools belong in the journey?

A structural analysis of housing rent‑vs‑buy (≡ invest‑vs‑buy) and renovate‑vs‑new comparison inside Plinthly's five‑phase funnel — not a UI design.

📅 2026‑06‑25 🧭 Senior PD / IA lens ⚙️ Grounded in the codebase
KNOW — from code, data & docs (cited) INFER — reasoned claim about users, flagged for research

The boundary up front

The brief states "user interviews surfaced demand for comparison tools." I could not verify this in the repo — what exists is a planned generative study (research/cuj-foundational-study-plan.md) and EN/DE interview scripts, all dated 2026‑06‑12, with the consolidated journey map listed as an intended output. So the research is designed but, on the repo evidence, not yet run. I treat "comparison is in demand" as an accepted premise, and treat where it fits as exactly the question that study should answer. Every user‑journey claim below is therefore INFER.

1

Information Architecture Audit

What the product is, the implicit hierarchy, and where the funnel serves vs. fights users.

Live app: a strictly linear 5‑phase funnel (App.jsx · PHASE_NUMBERS=[1‑5], LAST_PHASE=5). Docs still say "4‑phase" — the funnel already stretched once.

Implicit hierarchy & the core tension KNOW

Qualification is the root and the gate. goToPhase refuses any phase > 1 without a result (App.jsx:174); PhaseNav only allows jumps to already‑visited phases (maxVisited). Everything downstream inherits Phase 1's budget (explore.budget ← phase1.maxPrice, App.jsx:176‑182).

Two different jobs wear one navigation model. Phase 1 is qualification — deterministic, regulation‑bound, one correct answer; a funnel fits. Phases 2–4 are exploration — open‑ended, many valid answers — yet they're forced into the same gated, one‑way flow. That is where comparison fights the IA: rent‑vs‑buy/invest is, for many users, a question prior to qualification, and the funnel cannot express "I haven't decided I want to buy yet." INFER

◐ Redundancy — two parallel "go forward" mechanisms

Each result screen ships both a linear Continue → button and a NextSteps module whose cards route to Phase 3 & 4 (AffordabilityResult.jsx:577, DreamPricePhase.jsx:497). The product already has a modular contextual cross‑link pattern that jumps linear order — it just duplicates Continue rather than offering what the funnel can't. This is the key existing asset for housing comparison.

◇ Coherence gap — three budget‑like numbers, loosely tied

maxPrice (P1), dreamPrice (P2, a separate state, App.jsx:61), and explore.budget (P3, freely editable) aren't reconciled. Any comparison that asks "buy what, exactly?" must pick one anchor.

✕ Gap A — Rent‑vs‑buy / invest is entirely absent (incl. in the data)

No rent / opportunity‑cost / invest‑the‑difference logic anywhere. The building block exists — monthlyCostsAtRate gives real monthly carry (affordability.js:400) — but there's no rent figure in the dataset (only buy CHF/m², exploration.js), no equity‑accumulation projection, no investment‑return model. A data gap, not just a UI gap.

◑ Gap B — Renovate‑vs‑new is ~80% built, mis‑framed & buried

options.js + Phase3Options already render a full comparison (get‑in price · energy class · 10‑yr heating · trade‑off, Phase3Options.jsx:64‑90, 271‑326). But it's framed as "pick a route to execute" (selects chosenOption), not "is the difference substantial?" — and it sits four gated steps deep.

Mental Models & JTBD

All INFER, grounded in the brief's stated audience ("financially literate but rule‑illiterate") & Swiss market structure — the hypotheses the planned study should test.

Four mental models buyers bring

🚪 "Can I clear the hurdle?" → matches Phase 1 (correct root) ⚖️ "Rent or buy — is it worth it?" → bookends: early & late 📈 "Invest instead / stay liquid" → same model, most at odds with funnel 🔧 "Renovate or pay up for new?" → within‑buying, magnitude judgment

The conflict: rent‑vs‑buy and invest‑vs‑buy are one engine (rent + invest‑the‑difference vs. buy + build‑equity); only the vocabulary differs. It's the model most in tension with the funnel, because the funnel's premise is "you want to buy — here's how," and this user hasn't accepted that premise.

Core jobs & the comparison that belongs to each

JTBDServed today?Comparison that belongs
J1 "Am I even in the game?"Yes · Phase 1—
J2 "Is buying worth it vs. rent / invest?"NoRent‑vs‑buy / invest
J3 "What does my money buy, what's good?"Yes · Phase 3—
J4 "Which route to ownership?"Partial · Phase 4Renovate‑vs‑new "is it substantial?"
J5 "What do I do next?"Yes · Phase 5—

Is "compare" a destination, a mode, or an interruption?

  • Rent‑vs‑buy / invest = a MODE — a lens you switch into at decision moments. Best as a contextual module at two bookend moments, plus one thin standalone door for the pre‑qualification browser (the only persona the funnel can't serve).
  • Renovate‑vs‑new = a contextual module already living in the right phase.
  • Neither should be the primary "Compare hub" destination. A hub reads clean on a sitemap but fragments the journey, re‑poses inputs the funnel already has, and creates a second app to maintain.

Placement Options + Recommendation

Three concrete IA options, each weighed against the mental‑model & journey findings.

Option 1

A new comparison phase

"Is it worth it?" inserted between qualify & explore.

  • Explicit, discoverable, inherits state
  • Forces the lens on everyone
  • Lengthens a funnel already grown 4→5
  • Reframes a mode as a mandatory step
  • Still behind the gate — no help for the browser
Option 2

A parallel "Compare" hub

Second top‑level destination beside the funnel.

  • Serves the pre‑qualification browser
  • Home for future comparisons
  • Fragments the journey into two apps
  • Cold‑start inputs → the 7‑toggle problem returns
  • Duplicates state; maintenance cost
★ Recommended · Option 3

Contextual modules + one thin door

Modules at decision moments; a single standalone entry.

  • Matches the mental‑model finding (mode, bookends)
  • Reuses seeded state → kills toggle bloat
  • Preserves the funnel for serious buyers
  • Reuses existing NextSteps pattern
  • Comparison logic spread across surfaces

Recommended structure — in three moves

  • 1 · Rent‑vs‑buy / invest as a collapsible module on the Phase 1 result (optionally the Phase 2 dream result), seeded from maxPrice, monthly, mortgage, downPayment, canton. Reuses the NextSteps precedent and the existing market‑rate slider.
  • 2 · One low‑input standalone door from the Phase 1 "Other tracks" area ("Rent or buy? Start here") for the un‑qualified browser — generic, seeded only by canton, loudly flagged as indicative, one click into Phase 1 to personalize.
  • 3 · Renovate‑vs‑new stays in Phase 4, re‑framed — keep the ComparisonSummary, add an explicit verdict line ("buying new costs ~CHF X more to get in but saves ~CHF Y over 10 yrs; the gap is small/large") instead of a neutral row‑picker.

Defeating the toggle problem (the prototype's 7+ inputs)

① Lead with a verdict, hide assumptions (progressive disclosure) ② Smart defaults seeded from prior phases — zero new inputs to get an answer ③ Presets over sliders — "Cautious 2% / Balanced 4% / Optimistic 6%" × "Stay 5/10/20 yrs"

⚠︎ Honesty guardrail & data gap — must flag

Rent‑vs‑buy is far more assumption‑sensitive than renovate‑vs‑new (which is fully data‑backed today). It needs two things the dataset lacks: a rent figure (no cantonal rent CHF/m² in swiss-cantonal-data.json) and an assumed investment return. To honor "honest, indicative numbers," lead with a band + verdict (never a false‑precision point estimate) and carry a "this flips if…" sentence — mirroring the existing Indicative labelling. If no defensible rent benchmark can be sourced, require a user‑entered rent rather than inventing one.

Open Questions for Research

These map onto the planned (not‑yet‑run) CUJ study — several are already in its RQ set.

  1. Entry vs. validation? Is rent‑vs‑buy the first question or a late gut‑check for this audience? Decides whether the standalone door is essential. (study RQ1, RQ3)
  2. "Rent vs buy" or "invest vs buy"? Same model, different vocabulary — which entry label converts?
  3. What magnitude reads as "substantial" for renovate‑vs‑new? Sets the verdict‑line threshold.
  4. Will the un‑qualified browser accept a generic comparison, or does lost personalization erode the trust asset? (RQ4)
  5. Presets vs. sliders — do presets satisfy the financially‑literate user, or will they demand (and churn on) the raw controls?
  6. Funnel impact — does inserting comparison reduce completion of the qualify→action path? A guardrail metric.
  7. Sequencing — before or after the dream price, given the stretch‑number risk? (RQ6)

In one paragraph

Plinthly's funnel correctly models qualification as a gated, single‑answer flow, but forces exploration & comparison — modes with many valid answers — to live as gated children of it. Renovate‑vs‑new is already built but framed as a route‑picker rather than a "is the gap substantial?" verdict, and buried at Phase 4. Rent‑vs‑buy / invest is absent — and is the case most at odds with the funnel, because it's the question of users who haven't yet accepted that they want to buy. The recommended structure is contextual comparison modules at the two natural decision moments, plus one thin standalone door for the pre‑qualification browser — reusing the existing NextSteps pattern and seeded state to kill the toggle problem — not a new linear phase or a separate hub. The principal risk is data/assumption honesty: rent‑vs‑buy needs a rent benchmark the dataset lacks and an investment‑return assumption, both of which must surface as indicative bands with an explicit "this flips if…" caveat to protect the brand's core trust asset.

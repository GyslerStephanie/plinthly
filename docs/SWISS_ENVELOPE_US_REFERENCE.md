# Swiss building quality in the US: envelope, walls, windows, foundations, HVAC, water, floor heating

_Reference for the Plinthly resilient-build track. Draft 2026-10-12. Links were checked on that date. Figures marked (est.) are working estimates, not sourced data. Costs are 2026 US dollars, hard cost only._

## How to read a Swiss spec in US units

Swiss and European specs give U-values in W/m²K. US specs give R-values in hr·ft²·°F/BTU. Divide 5.678 by the U-value to get the R-value.

| Swiss target | U (W/m²K) | US equivalent | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minergie-P or Passivhaus wall | 0.10 to 0.15 | R-38 to R-57 | [BFE thermal-bridge catalogue for Minergie-P](https://pubdb.bfe.admin.ch/de/publication/download/3342), [BFE study on slim high-insulation walls](https://pubdb.bfe.admin.ch/de/publication/download/2956) |
| Conventional Swiss wall | ~0.30 | R-19 | same study |
| Minergie-P window, installed | ≤ 0.80 | U-0.14 (US) | Minergie-P module list |
| Airtightness, Minergie-P and Passivhaus | n50 ≤ 0.6 /h | 0.6 ACH50 | Minergie / PHI |
| 2021 IECC wall, US climate zone 5 | ~0.28 | R-20 cavity or R-13 + R-5 continuous | IECC |
| 2021 IECC airtightness, zones 3 to 8 | — | ≤ 3 ACH50 | IECC |

A Swiss Minergie-P wall built conventionally runs about 50 cm (20 in) thick; slimmer systems reach the target at 22 to 44 cm, according to the BFE study above. That is the first thing a US builder has to accept: the wall is twice as thick as what they know.

## Where the Swiss details live

- **[Minergie](https://www.minergie.ch/)** (DE, FR, IT; [minergie.com](https://www.minergie.com/) for the international site). Standards, module lists for windows and walls, certified-product lists. The [Minergie-P window and sliding-door module list](https://www.minergie.ch/media/180110_liste_minergie-p-modul_fenster_hebe-_schiebetueren.pdf) is a useful example of what "certified" means in Switzerland.
- **[Bauteilkatalog](https://www.bauteilkatalog.ch/)**, the Swiss federal building-component catalogue funded by the Federal Office of Energy (BFE) and ecobau. Layer-by-layer wall, roof and floor assemblies with U-values and grey energy. The single best source for "what is inside a Swiss wall".
- **[BFE U-value catalogue for renovations](https://www.fr.ch/sites/default/files/contens/sde/_www/files/pdf67/U-Wert-Berechnung_und_Bauteilekatalog_Sanierungen_d.pdf)** (PDF) and the **[Minergie-P thermal-bridge catalogue](https://pubdb.bfe.admin.ch/de/publication/download/3342)** (PDF). The thermal-bridge catalogue is the one to hand an American architect: it shows how Swiss builders detail window-to-wall, slab edge and roof-to-wall joints.
- **[Lignum](https://www.lignum.ch/)**, the Swiss timber industry association. The Lignumdata component catalogue (sound, fire, assemblies), the Lignatec series including [Fassaden von Holzbauten](https://www.lignum.ch/files/images/Downloads_deutsch/Fassaden_von_Holzbauten.pdf) (PDF, timber façades), and fire documentation for timber exterior walls.
- **[Holzbau Schweiz](https://www.holzbau-schweiz.ch/)**, the Swiss timber-builders' association. Member directory, which is where to find a Swiss Holzbau firm willing to advise or ship panels.
- **[Passive House Institute component database](https://database.passivehouse.com/)**. Certified wall systems, windows, doors, ventilation units and heat pumps with their tested values, filterable by climate zone. Both Swiss and US products appear here.

## The US detail libraries that say the same thing

Construction details for insulation thicker than 1.5 in with cladding attached outside it. Free PDF.
- **[Rockwool Comfortboard 80 application guide](https://www.idi-insulation.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/ROCKWOOL_COMFORTBOARD_ApplicationGuideENG_Web.pdf)** and **[residential installation guide](https://www.rockwool.com/syssiteassets/o2-rockwool/documentation/technical-guides/residential/comfortboard-80-residential-installation-guide.pdf)** (PDFs). Enclosure detail drawings by climate zone, cladding attachment, corners, penetrations.
- **[Fine Homebuilding: thick walls, faster, cheaper, better insulated](https://www.finehomebuilding.com/project-guides/framing/thick-walls-faster-cheaper-better-insulated)** and **[Lstiburek's ideal double-stud wall](https://www.finehomebuilding.com/project-guides/framing/lstibureks-ideal-double-stud-wall)**. The double-stud wall explained by the people who build it.
- **[Green Building Advisor](https://www.greenbuildingadvisor.com/)**. The US forum where every one of these assemblies has been argued over, with costs. Partly paywalled.
- **[PHIUS: find a certified professional](https://phius.org/find-professional)**. Consultants (CPHC), builders (CPHB), raters and verifiers by state.
- **[475 High Performance Building Supply](https://475.supply/)**, Brooklyn. The US importer of Pro Clima membranes and tapes (Intello, Solitex), with wall assembly guides and details written for US crews. The closest thing to a Swiss building-physics supplier in the US.

## Six walls, layer by layer

Each wall is listed from outside to inside. "Added cost" is per square foot of wall over a 2021-code 2x6 wall, or as a share of total house cost, all (est.). Whole-wall R-values account for framing.

### Swiss reference walls, for comparison

| Swiss wall | Layers (outside to inside) | Thickness | U / R |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timber frame, Holzrahmenbau | Wood or fibre-cement cladding, ventilated cavity, wood-fibre board 60 to 100 mm, 240 to 300 mm frame with cellulose or mineral wool, OSB air barrier, service cavity, gypsum fibre | 36 to 45 cm | 0.10 to 0.13 / R-44 to R-57 |
| Brick with compact façade, Kompaktfassade | Mineral render, 240 to 300 mm mineral wool or EPS (ETICS), 175 mm brick or sand-lime block, plaster | 45 to 50 cm | 0.10 to 0.12 / R-47 to R-57 |
| Concrete with ventilated façade, hinterlüftete Fassade | Cladding, ventilated cavity, 240 to 300 mm mineral wool, 200 mm concrete, plaster | 50 to 55 cm | 0.10 to 0.13 / R-44 to R-57 |

Sources for the layer logic: [Bauteilkatalog](https://www.bauteilkatalog.ch/), the BFE slim-wall study above, and the Rockwool [ventilated timber-frame façade detail](https://www.rockwool.com/syssiteassets/rw-d/detailzeichnungen/details-fassade---vhf/holzrahmenbau---anschluss-ortgang/detailplaner-aussenwand-hinterlueftete-fassade-im-holzrahmenbau-anschluss-ortgang-rockwool.pdf) (German, PDF).

### 1. 2x6 frame with exterior mineral wool

The US version of the Swiss ventilated façade. Also the only assembly here that works as a retrofit over an existing house.

| | |
|---|---|
| Layers | Fibre cement, wood or metal siding; 3/4 in vertical strapping (ventilated rainscreen); 3 to 4 in Rockwool Comfortboard 80; self-adhered or fluid-applied water and air barrier on plywood sheathing; 2x6 at 24 in o.c. with dense-pack cellulose or mineral wool batts; smart vapour retarder (Intello) or painted gypsum in mild climates; gypsum |
| Thickness | 11 to 12 in |
| Whole-wall R | R-30 to R-36 (2x6 cavity R-20 to R-23 plus R-12 to R-17 exterior) |
| Materials | Rockwool Comfortboard 80, long screws (Heco Topix or GRK), Pro Clima or Henry membranes, standard lumber |
| Added cost | $8 to $15 per sq ft of wall (est.). The board, the screws and a crew's first-time learning curve |
| Efficiency | Non-combustible, vapour-open both ways, kills thermal bridging at studs, keeps sheathing warm so it never condenses. Does not reach Minergie-P on its own; add a 2x8 cavity or 5 in of board to get there |
| How in the US | Any framing crew. Details in the Rockwool guides above and BSI-001. The cladding contractor needs the attachment table from the application guide. Comfortboard 80 is stocked by Rockwool distributors nationwide, 4 and 5 in thicknesses exist. Wood-fibre board (TimberHP TimberBoard, below) is the vapour-open alternative |
| Climate | All US zones. In hot-humid zones the vapour retarder goes outside or is omitted; follow the climate-zone pages of the application guide |

### 2. Double-stud wall with dense-pack cellulose

The standard Passive House wall in New England and the Pacific Northwest. Cheapest route to R-40 and beyond.

| | |
|---|---|
| Layers | Siding; strapping (rainscreen); vapour-open WRB (Solitex Mento or similar) on taped plywood or ZIP sheathing as the air barrier; outer 2x4 wall; 3 to 5 in gap; inner 2x4 wall; the whole 12 in cavity dense-packed with cellulose; Intello smart vapour retarder; service cavity optional; gypsum |
| Thickness | 13 to 14 in |
| Whole-wall R | R-40 to R-45 at 12 in ([GBA estimate](https://www.greenbuildingadvisor.com/question/double-stud-wall-with-mineral-wool-batts)) |
| Materials | Ordinary 2x4 lumber, cellulose, one membrane, tapes. No special products |
| Added cost | $3 to $6 per sq ft of wall (est.). Fine Homebuilding's builders priced an R-45 double-stud wall 14 percent below an R-40 2x6 wall with spray foam and 2 in exterior foam ([source](https://www.finehomebuilding.com/project-guides/framing/thick-walls-faster-cheaper-better-insulated)). A Montana quote put the double-stud framing and insulation at $39,000 against $44,500 for 2x6 plus 4 in exterior XPS ([GBA thread](https://www.greenbuildingadvisor.com/question/cost-of-a-double-stud-wall-with-dense-packed-cellulose)) |
| Efficiency | Reaches Minergie-P levels with cheap materials. Carbon-storing cellulose. The risk is the cold outer sheathing in late winter; the smart vapour retarder and a vapour-open WRB manage it, and Lstiburek's version in Fine Homebuilding addresses it |
| How in the US | Any framing crew. The constraint is a dense-pack installer who has done a 12 in cavity; get a bid before committing. Lstiburek's detail and the FHB article are the drawings |
| Climate | Cold and mixed zones (4 to 7). In hot-humid zones prefer wall 1 or 4 |

### 3. Insulated concrete forms (ICF)

The nearest US equivalent to Swiss concrete construction. The natural wall for Florida, Texas and the Gulf.

| | |
|---|---|
| Layers | Stucco, fibre cement or brick veneer; 2.5 to 2.75 in EPS (form face); 6 or 8 in reinforced concrete core; 2.5 to 2.75 in EPS; furring and gypsum. For Minergie-P levels add 2 to 3 in of exterior mineral wool or use the thicker-foam forms (Nudura XR35) |
| Thickness | 11 to 13 in, 14 to 16 in with added exterior insulation |
| Whole-wall R | R-22 to R-24 standard ([Nudura](https://nudura.com/resources/r-value-thermal-mass), [CLEB test at -31°F](https://www.nudura.com/blog/what-is-r-value-what-is-thermal-mass)); up to R-35 to R-48 with XR35 or Plus forms; mass effect adds real performance in cooling climates |
| Materials | Nudura, Fox Blocks, Amvic, BuildBlock forms; ready-mix concrete; rebar. All stocked regionally |
| Added cost | 5 to 10 percent on total house cost (est.); a GBA poster was quoted $16,000 for R-25 forms on one house and about double for R-35 ([thread](https://www.greenbuildingadvisor.com/question/is-it-necessary-to-go-beyond-the-standard-icf-r23)) |
| Efficiency | Airtight by nature (one blower-door pass is routine), hurricane and tornado rated, non-combustible core, quiet, termite-detailed. The foam is not vapour-open, so keep the concrete dry by detailing the base and the top of wall |
| How in the US | ICF crews exist in every hazard state; the manufacturers run installer training and keep installer directories. Window bucks and connection details come from the manufacturer's detail library. Code path: IRC Section R611 / ICC-ES reports |
| Climate | All zones; best value in hot-humid, hurricane and wildfire areas |

### 4. Autoclaved aerated concrete (AAC) block

The Ytong and Hebel system used across Switzerland and Germany, made in the US by one plant: [AERCON AAC](https://www.aerconaac.com/) in Haines City, Florida.

| | |
|---|---|
| Layers | Mineral render or stucco (or 2 to 4 in exterior mineral wool plus render for Minergie-P levels); 8, 10 or 12 in AAC block laid in thin-bed mortar; interior plaster or gypsum |
| Thickness | 9 to 13 in; 12 to 16 in with exterior insulation |
| Whole-wall R | About R-11.5 for 8 in and R-17 for 12 in of block alone ([GBA, citing Aercon](https://www.greenbuildingadvisor.com/question/new-home-aac-zone-6b)); add R-8 to R-16 of exterior mineral wool to reach R-25 to R-33. Mass performance in cooling climates is better than the number |
| Materials | AERCON block, panels, thin-bed mortar; render systems from Sto, Parex |
| Added cost | 5 to 12 percent on house (est.); a north-Florida builder put a one-storey AAC house at 2.5 percent over stud and stucco ([This Old House](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/masonry/autoclaved-aerated-concrete-aac)) |
| Efficiency | Fireproof, termite-proof, does not grow mould, vapour-open, solid and quiet. Lower R per inch than foam, so it needs exterior insulation to hit Swiss numbers |
| How in the US | Masons already know block; AAC needs a short training on thin-bed mortar. Code path: [ICC-ES ESR-1652](https://icc-es.org/wp-content/uploads/report-directory/ESR-1652.pdf) with an engineered design under IRC R301.1.3. Finding an experienced residential AAC sub is the hard part; forum reports say electricians and plumbers upcharge the first time |
| Climate | Hot-humid and hurricane zones first (Florida, Gulf, Southeast); works anywhere with exterior insulation |

### 5. Prefab timber panels or CLT with wood-fibre insulation

This is Swiss Holzbau. Factory-built, two weeks to weathertight, and the one that transfers the Swiss trade most directly.

| | |
|---|---|
| Layers | Cladding; ventilated cavity; 2 to 6 in wood-fibre board (TimberHP TimberBoard, Gutex Multitherm); either a deep timber-frame panel (10 to 14 in, cellulose-filled, OSB air barrier inside) or a 3-ply CLT panel (3.5 to 4 in) as structure and air barrier; service cavity; gypsum or exposed wood |
| Thickness | 14 to 20 in |
| Whole-wall R | R-35 to R-50 depending on the frame depth and board thickness |
| Materials | Wood-fibre board: [TimberHP](https://www.timberhp.com/), Madison, Maine, production since late 2025 ([ESR-5387](https://www.arcat.com/certificates/timberhp/ESR_5387.pdf)); Gutex imported via 475. CLT: see the WoodWorks and Think Wood lists below |
| Added cost | 15 to 35 percent on house for CLT (est.), 5 to 15 percent for prefab frame panels, less once the shorter site schedule is priced in |
| Efficiency | Vapour-open all the way through, large carbon storage, airtight from the factory, very good summer comfort from the mass of the wood-fibre board and the CLT |
| How in the US | Order the panels. [Bensonwood](https://bensonwood.com/) and [Unity Homes](https://unityhomes.com/) (Keene, NH; R-35 walls, 0.6 ACH50 target, PHlex Passive House panels), [Ecocor](https://www.ecocor.us/) (Searsmont, ME; Passive House prefab, Viking windows), [Build SMART](https://www.buildsmartna.com/) (PHI-certified panel system with slab forms, ships to the eastern US and Canada). CLT manufacturers: [WoodWorks](https://www.woodworks.org/) and [Think Wood](https://www.thinkwood.com/) keep the lists; single-family CLT is still a niche and priced as one |
| Climate | All zones; the ventilated cladding handles rain, the wood-fibre board handles summer heat |

### 6. Structural insulated panels (SIPs)

A cheaper middle ground outside wildfire zones.

| | |
|---|---|
| Layers | Cladding; rainscreen; WRB; OSB skin; 6.5 to 12 in EPS or polyurethane core; OSB skin; gypsum. Add exterior mineral wool for Minergie-P levels |
| Thickness | 8 to 13 in |
| Whole-wall R | R-25 to R-45 |
| Materials | Insulspan, Premier SIPs, Enercept, Thermocore |
| Added cost | 5 to 10 percent on house (est.) |
| Efficiency | Airtight when the joints are sealed properly, fast, few thermal bridges. The core is foam, not vapour-open, and the OSB skins must never get wet. Avoid in wildfire zones |
| How in the US | Panel makers supply shop drawings and an installer network. The one skill to insist on: taping and sealing every panel joint before the cladding goes on |

### What every wall needs regardless

- A blower-door test at rough-in and at completion. Target 0.6 ACH50 for the Swiss grade, 1.0 to 1.5 for a strong retrofit.
- Windows installed in the insulation layer, not at the frame, with the thermal-bridge catalogue's "over-insulated frame" detail.
- A roof built to the same standard: R-60 plus, and for hazard states a Class 4 shingle or standing-seam metal roof to IBHS FORTIFIED.
- A service cavity inside the air barrier so electricians never pierce it.

## Foundations

Swiss houses sit on insulated basements or slabs with perimeter insulation (Perimeterdämmung) in XPS or foam-glass gravel (Misapor, Glapor), and the slab edge is detailed in the thermal-bridge catalogue. The US tools for the same job:

- **Frost-protected shallow foundation (FPSF).** IRC R403.3 and ASCE 32. A monolithic slab with 4 to 8 in of EPS under it and insulated wings, so no frost wall and no basement. [Fine Homebuilding's cold-climate slab guide](https://www.finehomebuilding.com/project-guides/insulation/slab-foundation-cold-climates).
- **[Legalett](https://legalett.ca/)**, Long Sault, Ontario, serving Canada and the US. Engineered insulated slab (GEO-Slab) with an optional air-heated radiant loop cast into the slab; references Passive House projects.
- **[Build SMART](https://www.buildsmartna.com/)**. PHI-certified slab forms shipped with their wall panels.
- **Foam-glass gravel under the slab**, the Swiss Misapor method. [Glavel](https://www.glavel.com/), Vermont, and [AeroAggregates of North America](https://www.aeroaggna.com/), Eddystone, Pennsylvania. Replaces both the crushed stone and the foam; R-1.7 per inch compacted, about R-12 to R-16 at 12 in; $70 to $80 per cubic yard in 2019 reports. Not yet a listed FPSF insulation in the IRC, so an engineer signs off ([GBA](https://www.greenbuildingadvisor.com/question/concrete-free-slab-with-fpsf-and-pwf-using-glavel-second-attempt), [GBA on foamed glass](https://www.greenbuildingadvisor.com/article/foamed-glass-aggregate)).
- **ICF basements**, where a basement is wanted. Same forms as wall 3, with exterior waterproofing (Sika, Henry) and a drained footing.

## HVAC: ventilation, heating, hot water

Minergie makes mechanical ventilation with heat recovery (Komfortlüftung) mandatory, and the heat source is almost always a heat pump. In a wall-1-to-5 house the heating load is so small that the ventilation and the hot water matter more than the heater.

- **Ventilation (ERV/HRV):** [Zehnder America](https://zehnderamerica.com/), the US arm of the Swiss Zehnder Group. ComfoAir Q series, PHI and PHIUS listed, counter-flow cores at 84 to 92 percent. Alternatives: Broan-NuTone AI series, Renewaire, Ultimate Air. Design the duct runs with the unit maker; this is the part US HVAC contractors get wrong.
- **Space heating, air-to-air:** Mitsubishi H2i Hyper-Heat minisplits, rated to -13°F, the default in US Passive House projects ([Mitsubishi case](https://MITSUBISHICOMFORT.COM/sites/default/files/2021-11/Facility%20Management%20Newsletter_Volume6Issue4.pdf)). One or two heads heat a Swiss-grade house.
- **Space heating, air-to-water (for floor heating):** Chiltrix (monobloc, no refrigerant indoors), SpacePak, Arctic Heat Pumps, Nordic, Stiebel Eltron WPL, Aermec, Taco ([JLC overview](https://www.jlconline.com/how-to/hvac/air-to-water-heat-pumps-come-of-age_o/), [NYS Clean Heat qualified list](https://cleanheat.ny.gov/assets/pdf/NYS%20Clean%20Heat%20AWHP%20QPL%20-%20May-2-2026.pdf)). This is the closest thing to a Swiss Luft-Wasser-Wärmepumpe.
- **Ground source:** WaterFurnace, Bosch, ClimateMaster. The Swiss Erdsonde; justified in the US only where drilling is cheap.
- **Hot water:** heat-pump water heaters. [Stiebel Eltron USA](https://stiebel-eltron-usa.com/water-heating), Hatfield, Massachusetts (Accelera 300), Rheem, A.O. Smith. In a Passive-grade house the hot-water heater is the biggest energy user.
- **Controls:** [Belimo Americas](https://www.belimo.com/), Swiss, with plants in Danbury, Connecticut and Sparks, Nevada. Valves and actuators for anyone doing a proper hydronic system.

## Floor heating

Swiss houses run hydronic floor heating in a screed at 28 to 35°C (82 to 95°F) supply, which is exactly the temperature a heat pump likes. US products that do the same:

- **In-slab or in-screed PEX:** [Uponor](https://www.uponor.com/en-us) (Wirsbo), Rehau, Viega. The Swiss method, embedded in the slab from the foundations section.
- **Low-mass panels for upper floors and retrofits:** [Warmboard](https://www.warmboard.com/products/panels/), aluminium-faced subfloor with 12 in tube spacing, designed for supply below 120°F and typically under 100°F; Viega Climate Panel.
- **Air-heated slab:** Legalett, above.
- **Design rule:** pair with an air-to-water heat pump on outdoor reset, size for 95 to 105°F supply, and have a hydronic designer check the output at that temperature ([GBA discussion](https://greenbuildingadvisor.com/question/hydronic-radiant-floor-heat-with-heatpump)). In a Swiss-grade envelope floor heating is a comfort choice, not a necessity; many US Passive House projects skip it for a minisplit and the ERV.

## Water: rain, grey water, fixtures

- **Rainwater:** the Swiss market leader Graf has no US distributor. US equivalents: [Rainwater Management Solutions](https://rainwatermanagement.com/pages/residential-consulting-and-design), Virginia, exclusive North American distributor of the German WISY filters, turnkey residential systems; BRAE (a Watts company), 225 to 3,000 gallon residential tanks; RainHarvest Systems; RainFlo packages around $6,700 for 1,500 gallons ([Rise review](https://buildwithrise.com/stories/review-top-rainwater-harvesting-systems)). State rules vary; Plinthly's US data already carries each state's rainwater rule.
- **Grey water:** [Greyter](https://greyter.com/) HOME, NSF 350 certified, sold through Ferguson as exclusive residential distributor, used by Lennar and KB Home; [Hydraloop](https://www.hydraloop.com/), Dutch with a US office, units from about $2,000, 25 to 45 percent water savings in its California case study. Both need a state that permits indoor grey-water reuse (California, Arizona, Texas, Washington lead).
- **Fixtures:** Geberit (Swiss) and Laufen sell in the US through Geberit North America; Grohe and Hansgrohe (German) are everywhere. [Sika USA](https://usa.sika.com/) for waterproofing and the Swiss habit of a properly sealed wet room.

## Making it possible in the US: the sequence

1. **Pick the wall by state, not by preference.** Florida, Texas, Gulf: ICF or AAC with exterior mineral wool. Colorado, Northeast, Northwest: double-stud or 2x6 plus exterior mineral wool, or prefab timber panels. California wildfire zones: ICF, AAC, or 2x6 plus mineral wool, never SIPs or wood-fibre board exposed.
2. **Hire the two people who make it work:** a PHIUS Certified Consultant to run the energy model and the thermal-bridge details, and a builder who has passed a blower-door test at 1.0 ACH50 or better. Both are in the [PHIUS directory](https://phius.org/find-professional).
3. **Buy the envelope as a package where you can.** Bensonwood, Ecocor and Build SMART deliver walls, roof and slab forms as one system, which removes most of the site-crew risk.
4. **Use the US code paths that already exist.** IRC R403.3 for the slab, IRC R611 and ICC-ES reports for ICF, ESR-1652 for AAC, ESR-5387 for TimberBoard, NFRC labels for windows. None of this needs a variance.
5. **Spend the money in this order:** airtightness and window installation detail (cheap, decisive), exterior continuous insulation, triple-pane windows, ERV, then the heat pump. Finishes last.
6. **Build smaller.** The envelope premium is per square foot of wall and window; a 1,800 sq ft house at Swiss grade costs about what a 2,800 sq ft code house costs (est.), which is the comparison the Plinthly explorer exists to show.

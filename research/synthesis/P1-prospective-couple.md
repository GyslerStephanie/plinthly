# P1 synthesis — prospective-buyer couple (pilot session)

**Segment:** A (prospective, ~1 year into "serious"). Couple: she = architect (HU), he = physicist, federal sector (DK). Renting in Zurich, searching Kanton Aargau. Convenience sample (friends) — flag politeness bias, though they were notably candid/pessimistic.

## Trigger
Family members buying property in home countries (~1 year ago) + cultural norm: "It's the natural next step... establish a family, you get married, you have a house." Considered investment flats in Hungary/Denmark first; rejected to stay liquid for a Swiss purchase ("several hundreds of thousands of francs").

## Journey steps (their sequence)
1. **Learn baseline rules** — via family comparison + general knowledge. Swiss rules perceived as a *strength*: "In Switzerland it's always really, really clear" (vs. Denmark: no minimum, just convince the bank).
2. **Rough affordability estimate** from salary + savings — "only these 2 things and we already know these things."
3. **Browse listings** (portals, filters, Aargau) → **the reality check**: "you realize what you *cannot* afford... hurtful." Within budget: only "houses with potential" (full renovations) or off-plan Neubau (~30% cheaper, but "you pay a lot of money for something that exists in 3 years").
4. **Play with scenarios** — higher price points, "how much longer would we have to keep saving." Refuse to max out budget: "you have to have the safety net."
5. **Stall.** Never visited a single property. "We only talk about ads, so to say."

## Tools & the core frustration ★
Google "mortgage calculator" → first results; also ChatGPT. Verbatim, near-exact articulation of Plinthly's reverse mode:

> "You put in the property value... and I don't understand why that is the starting point, because that is the irrelevant number. The relevant number is what do we have and what can we afford... I put in 1.8 million, it says no. 1.7, still no. Okay, continue... **It should be the other way around.**"

Plus an unbuilt extension — **time-to-affordability projection**:

> "My salary is going to increase like this... we save this much per year. When were we able to afford it if we wait 1, 2, 3, 5 years?" (with assumable property-price inflation, e.g. 2%/yr)

And the honest endpoint: "maybe I realize I will *never* afford the quality I want — and therefore it makes sense *not* to buy." They want that answer too.

## Knowledge gaps & how they're filled
- Pillar 2 usability discovered "very late in the process."
- Renovation tax deductions: learned only via his boss. Pattern: knowledge diffuses through **older colleagues (~10-yr gap), family, workplace** — not tools. "If you don't know what to study, you cannot self-study. You need an entry point."

## Sustainability / subsidies (probed, not spontaneous)
Aware subsidies exist; never calculated them. "Too opaque... such a jungle" — federal/cantonal/**Gemeinde**-level variation kills confidence (she knows this professionally). Not a blocker, but unbudgetable → default behavior: **assume zero subsidies** ("easier to be pessimistic"), even where "subsidies would in some cases be what makes it affordable." Would engage if quantified per property — and: "It doesn't even have to be an accurate calculation. It could also just be *advice*."

## Rent-vs-buy reasoning (sophisticated)
Opportunity cost of down payment in stocks "typically just as good, if not better"; monthly ownership cost ≈ rent once interest+amortization+maintenance counted; maintenance "often hidden"; resale/downsizing at pension as exit. Also: moving somewhere cheap to save is "not a viable strategy" — CHF 1'000/month = only 120k/10yrs at a big quality-of-life cost.

## Attitude to pension money
He: cynical on retirement age ("won't stay 65") → comfortable using pillars. Notes peers split on this — worth probing per participant.

## Magic wand
- Him: "Not how fast can I reach it — **is it even feasible within a reasonable limit?**" Wants to preview paths + what each costs in life terms ("I need 2 vacations a year") before committing 5 years of sacrifice.
- Both: an **honest, adversarial advisor** — "ChatGPT is always on your side. I don't want positivity." Must surface cons (bus once a day, need a car, school quality by Gemeinde) and unknown-unknowns: "You don't know what to ask... basically what you need is automatic prompt engineering."
- Location intelligence gap: schools, transport, taxes, commute — "the data is probably somewhere, but nobody collects it."

## Product implications (Plinthly)
| Finding | Implication |
|---|---|
| Reverse-calc frustration, verbatim | Core value prop validated by pilot — lead with it |
| Salary/savings projection ask | Feature gap: "affordable in N years" slider (savings rate, salary growth, price inflation) — arguably the strongest ask of the session |
| "I don't want positivity" | "Honest numbers / not yet" positioning validated |
| Subsidy jungle, assume-zero default | Phase 2/3 subsidy content valuable but frame as *guidance*, not precise promises; Gemeinde variance is the trust ceiling |
| Rent-vs-buy math | Candidate module: honest rent-vs-buy comparison incl. opportunity cost + maintenance |
| Entry-point problem / unknown-unknowns | Validates guided-flow + glossary approach; "TL;DR not 40 pages" |
| Never visited a property | The listings reality-check is where journeys stall — "what to look for" phase targets the right moment |

**Caveats:** n=1, couple interview, friends, both high-context (architect + finance-adjacent). Treat all as hypotheses until ≥3 participants converge.
